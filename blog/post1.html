Post 1: History of the Internet

January 29, 2026

Most days, the internet feels like magic: I tap a link, and a page just appears. But under the hood, it’s a lot more physical, messy, and honestly kind of charming.

A tiny “fail” that started everything

In 1969, a UCLA student named Charlie Kline tried to send the word “login” to developers at Stanford using ARPANET. The system crashed after the first two letters, so the first internet message was basically “lo.” I love that the internet’s origin story is a small failure that still counts as history.

Why the internet breaks everything into pieces

When you load a website, the information doesn’t travel as one big file. It gets broken into many small data packets, which travel across the internet to reach a server. Then the server sends packets back to your device. Your browser (Chrome, Safari, etc.) is the “client,” and the server is usually a computer in the cloud.

Here’s the fun part: each packet can take a different route. Routers decide where each packet goes next, so some packets arrive faster than others. Once everything arrives, the packets reassemble in the correct order—like a scattered puzzle snapping back into place.

TCP/IP: the “post office” and the “address”

TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) helps make sure data is delivered correctly and intact—kind of like a post office worker. IP (Internet Protocol) makes sure it goes to the correct destination, like an address on an envelope. Together, TCP/IP creates a communication line between devices and servers, and it works across four layers so different devices can still talk to each other.
